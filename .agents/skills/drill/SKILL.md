# Drill procedure (CCA-F)

Follow these steps exactly. They exist because models tend to (a) reveal the answer too early,
(b) skew the A/B/C/D distribution toward B/C and starve D, and (c) subtly telegraph the answer
through formatting or option length.

> This version has **no enforcement hook** — every rule here is a discipline you must hold
> yourself. The distribution rule in particular has no automatic audit, so plan it deliberately
> (step 1) rather than letting answers fall where they may.

## The one hard rule about leaks
Do not reveal a question's correct answer until the user has answered that question. Keep the
planned answer key in your own reasoning — do not print it, list it, or hint at it. State each
verdict only after the user has committed to an answer.

## 1. Plan the whole answer key first, in your reasoning
Before writing any questions, decide the correct-answer letter for every position. This up-front
plan is what prevents skew — if you assign answers on the fly, they drift toward B/C and D gets
starved. Aim for:
- Each of A/B/C/D used roughly evenly (for N=10: each 2–3 times; scale for other N).
- Every letter, including **D**, genuinely used — D is the one most often neglected.
- No run of the same letter 4+ times in a row.

Hold this sequence in your reasoning. Do NOT print, write, or echo it anywhere.

## 2. Plain-text stems
Write question stems in plain text. Do NOT bold or italicize any phrase that gives away the
answer. Save emphasis of the decisive cue for the explanation AFTER the user answers.

## 3. Balanced options (length symmetry + plausible distractors)
Within each question, make the four options hard to tell apart by shape alone:
- **Length symmetry:** keep all four roughly equal in length, sentence structure, and technical
  complexity. Never let the correct answer be the longest or most detailed — that leaks it.
- **Plausible distractors:** every wrong option should reflect a real misconception someone who
  half-knows the material might hold. No obvious throwaways or joke answers.

## 4. One question at a time
Present exactly one question with its four options, placing the correct option at the letter you
planned for that position. Stop and wait. Do not preview later questions or their answers.

## 5. Reveal only after the user answers
After the user answers, state right/wrong and explain the decisive cue — all from your own
reasoning, in prose. Then present the next question. Never reveal a future question's answer.

## 6. Never disclose the scheme
Never print the planned key, "the answers are…", a table of upcoming answers, or any slot before
its question has been answered. If you catch yourself about to, stop.
